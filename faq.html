Frequently Asked Questions

Updated October 22, 2026.

Is Rebate Atlas free?

Yes. There is no cost and no account. The site may show ads and may earn referral fees from some partner links in the future, which never affects which programs we list.

Can I still get the federal 25C or 25D tax credit in 2026?

No, not for new work. The 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit does not apply to property placed in service after December 31, 2025, and the 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit (solar, batteries, geothermal) does not apply to expenditures made after December 31, 2025. If your project was completed in 2025, you can still claim it on IRS Form 5695 with your 2025 return.

What about the EV charger and EV tax credits?

The 30C EV charger credit ended for chargers placed in service after June 30, 2026. The new and used clean vehicle credits (30D and 25E) ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. Some states and utilities still offer charger or EV rebates; check your state guide.

What incentives are still available in 2026?

The main ones are the IRA-funded HEAR (electrification) and HOMES (whole-home efficiency) rebates in states that have launched them, the free Weatherization Assistance Program for income-eligible households, state rebates and tax credits, and utility rebates for heat pumps, insulation, water heaters and smart thermostats.

Has my state launched HEAR or HOMES?

It depends on the state. As of the NASEO tracker updated September 30, 2026, HEAR had launched in Arizona, California, Colorado (single-family reported closed), D.C., Georgia, Indiana, Maine, Michigan, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Rhode Island, West Virginia and Wisconsin. Each state page shows its status and who to contact. Find your state.

Who qualifies for HEAR and HOMES rebates?

HEAR is for households at or below 150% of area median income: up to 100% of costs for households below 80% AMI and up to 50% for 80–150% AMI, with a cap of $14,000 per household. HOMES rebates are based on modeled or measured energy savings, with larger rebates for lower-income households. States set the details, so read your state program rules.

Do renters qualify for anything?

Often, yes. Some HEAR and HOMES programs allow renters or landlords to apply, weatherization programs serve renters with landlord permission, and many utility rebates cover portable or plug-in products. See our renter vs. homeowner guide. Read the renter guide.

Can I combine rebates?

Usually you can stack utility rebates with state or HEAR rebates, but some programs reduce their amount if another rebate pays for the same item, and the same cost can’t be paid twice. Our stacking guide explains the common rules. Read the stacking guide.

Where does your information come from?

Official sources: IRS guidance, the U.S. Department of Energy, the NASEO rebate tracker, state energy offices, utility program pages and DSIRE. Every program card links to its source.

How accurate is the information?

We verify against primary sources and date-stamp updates, but programs change and funding can run out. Always confirm on the official program page before you buy or install.

Is the AI assistant giving tax or legal advice?

No. It explains programs and helps you figure out what to ask, using current federal facts. Eligibility is decided by the program administrator, and tax questions should go to a tax professional.

We don’t sell personal information and we don’t install anything. We link to official programs and, where useful, to outside services you can choose to use.
